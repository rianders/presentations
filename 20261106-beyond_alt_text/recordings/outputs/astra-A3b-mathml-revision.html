5. “Different example”: a square-root denominator

Position and marks. Below the black wavy divider, “DIFFERENT EXAMPLE:” is written in black on the left. A red equality sits to its right.

2 x + 9 x 2 = 2 x + 9 x \frac{2x+9}{\sqrt{{x}^{2}}}=\frac{2x+9}{x}

Equation in words: (2 x plus 9) over the square root of x squared, equals (2 x plus 9) over x.

Transcriber’s note (not handwritten): The displayed equality assumes x > 0. In general, √(x²) = |x|, so the denominator is |x| for nonzero real x. This qualification is not written in the source.

Equation in words: [(2 x plus 9) times the fifth root of x cubed] over [the fifth root of x squared times the fifth root of x cubed], equals [(2 x plus 9) times the fifth root of x cubed] over the fifth root of x to the fifth.

Transcriber’s note (not handwritten): The first black line includes an extra (2x + 9) in the multiplier’s numerator. It is absent from the coloured line and subsequent work. The later steps are consistent with multiplying by only the fifth root of x³ over itself. The transcription retains the extra factor rather than silently correcting it.
