# AI-Assisted Digital Accessibility Workflows — The Hard Cases
**September 18, 2026 · UOES / TIIP Faculty Workshop · Rick Anderson**

**What I actually want from this doc: the material you cannot make accessible.**

Not the easy files. The one you have been avoiding — the thing where the checker is
no help, the built-in tools give you nothing usable, and you have quietly decided to
deal with it later. You are the ones living this. I have a pattern that works on a lot
of material, but I only find out where it breaks when someone shows me something that
breaks it.

Add yours below. Examples, links, questions — anything. You do not need to raise a
hand or unmute, and you do not need to wait for a particular moment in the session.

---

## What happens to what you post

Three outcomes, and I will tell you which one yours is rather than leaving you guessing:

1. **I answer it out loud today.** A lot of these turn out to be solved already, by
   something you are entitled to and did not know was there.
2. **We work it in the hands-on half.** If it needs hands on a keyboard, bring it to
   the second half of the session and we do it together.
3. **It is genuinely hard, so I take it away as an experiment and report back.** No
   invented answer in the room. If your case beats the pattern, that is the useful
   result, and I would rather find out in here than have you find out alone in March.

Anything unanswered when we finish stays in this doc, and I fill it in afterwards.

## The material you cannot make accessible

Copy the block, fill in what you can. Two or three lines is plenty — I will come back
to you for detail.

```
WHAT IT IS       (e.g. 40-page scanned chapter, lecture deck, lab figure, dataset)
WHAT BREAKS      (what you tried, and what it gave you)
LINK             (Canvas link, or "can't share" — see the note below)
YOU              (name, so I can come back to you — optional)
```

**Before you paste a link:** a licensed reading, anything with student work in it, or
anything you would not email outside Rutgers does not belong in a shared doc. Link to
it where it already lives in Canvas, or just describe it. "Can't share" is a completely
fine answer and does not make your case less useful.

### Seeded examples — this is the level of detail that helps

**WHAT IT IS** — A 40-page scanned chapter, photographed from a library book. Image only.
**WHAT BREAKS** — Ran it through a scanner app. The OCR turns the footnotes into
sentences and drops them into the middle of the paragraph above. Marginalia comes out as
nonsense words.
**LINK** — Can't share, licensed.
*(This one is probably lane 1. There is a Rutgers service for exactly this, and OCR
quality is a scan-quality problem before it is a tool problem.)*

**WHAT IT IS** — A 60-slide lecture deck, six years old, roughly 90 figures.
**WHAT BREAKS** — PowerPoint's alt-text generator writes something for every image, so
the checker goes green, and none of it says what the figure is for. Fixing 90 by hand is
not happening before April.
**LINK** — In my Canvas site, happy to share.
*(Lane 2. This is the whole vision segment and it is worth doing together — the useful
part is deciding which of the 90 actually carry information.)*

**WHAT IT IS** — Multi-panel figures from a journal article. Panel C refers back to panel A,
and the caption assumes you can see both at once.
**WHAT BREAKS** — Every description I get is accurate about each panel separately and
useless about the relationship, which is the entire point of the figure.
**LINK** — Can't share.
*(Lane 3, and an honest one. I do not have a pattern for cross-panel relationships that
I trust. This is the kind of case I would take away and actually test.)*

## Questions

Anything that is a question rather than a case. I read from this list and answer out
loud, so nothing gets lost in chat.

**Q. Do I have to fix everything by April 26, 2027?**
*(seeded — that is the deadline for WCAG 2.1 Level AA under the DOJ's April 2024 update
to ADA Title II, and it has already been extended once from April 2026. The honest answer
for most people is that you start with what students actually open.)*

**Q. Do I have to turn Ally on, or buy it?**
*(seeded — neither. Rutgers holds an enterprise license and it is already in every Canvas
course. Students can already download your files as audio, ePub, tagged PDF and braille
without asking you for anything.)*

**Q. Can I just let it auto-generate the alt text?**
*(seeded — it will, and a checker will go green, and a student still learns nothing. The
AI can describe what is in the image; the sentence about what the figure is FOR has to
come from you.)*
