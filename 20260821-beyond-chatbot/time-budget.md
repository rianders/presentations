## Ranking criteria

Each segment scored 0–3 on three axes. Higher total = more protected. Prune from the
bottom.

**A · Promised in the abstract.** Registrants read a specific description and signed up
on it. This is the closest thing to a contract, so it's weighted first.
- 3 = named explicitly as one of the four pillars
- 2 = described in the abstract's body
- 1 = gestured at
- 0 = not mentioned

**B · Sets up a work-session track.** The hands-on 30 min offers five tracks. If a
segment is the only place a track gets explained, cutting it breaks the work session.
- 3 = the track fails without it
- 1 = supports a track partially
- 0 = no track depends on it

**C · Timely and unique.** Can they get this from any other talk, blog post, or the
Mar 6 recording? Content that is days old and locally specific is the reason to attend
*this* session rather than read about it later.
- 3 = days old, Rutgers-specific
- 2 = weeks old, or locally framed
- 1 = widely available elsewhere
- 0 = they've heard it

### The tightest spot — resolved

Slide 5 previously held two separate polls plus a chat moment in 4 minutes; two Zoom
polls alone run ~3 min once you launch, wait, close, and share each one.

**Applied:** the two are now **one poll with two questions**, launched once — roughly
90 s instead of 3 min, which makes the 4-minute allocation workable alongside the chat
moment.

Consequence worth knowing: **anonymity in Zoom is a per-poll setting, not per-question.**
Merging therefore makes Q1 ("do you have a paid plan?") anonymous as well. That is fine
and arguably better — it removes any sense of being counted by name — but it does mean
you cannot follow up with a specific person based on their answer. The chat moment is
where named, voluntary detail comes from instead.

Deck now needs **three** Zoom polls built in the portal, not four. See the ZOOM PREP
block in `beyond-the-chatbot.jsx`.

**Why this beats plain Scenario 1.** Growing Vibe Coding to 14 buried the most important
idea inside a segment about something else. Splitting it out gives the pathway a title,
its own demo, and a place in the dot navigator — and it lets Local AI shrink to 4,
because "small models" now has a visible payoff two slides earlier instead of being a
standalone curiosity.

**The risk to watch.** Vibe Coding at 12 must still deliver the *promised* thing:
describe a Canvas page in plain English, get content back, no coding experience
required. If Path 2 eats that segment, the new slide is a bonus that cost you a
promise.

## Recommendation

**Scenario 1.** It keeps every promise in the published abstract, protects the two
segments that scored 8, and the two things it cuts hardest — Why Now and Free vs. Paid —
are the two lowest scorers, both discretionary.

The Why Now cut is the good trade specifically: a tool got renamed in July and two more
turned on Monday. That *is* the argument that things are moving. Eight minutes of
framing to establish what four days of news establishes on its own is the most expensive
low-value content in the deck.

## Decide before writing final content

Cutting slides now is cheap. Cutting paragraphs Thursday night is not — and Thursday
night is also when the demos need rehearsing.
