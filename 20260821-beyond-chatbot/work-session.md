## Format decision: main room, not breakouts — SETTLED

**Everyone stays in the main room.** You are hosting solo.

With five breakout rooms you can be in one at a time, which means four rooms sit
unsupported while you help one person — and the person you're helping gets a private
answer nobody else hears. In the main room, every question you answer out loud lands for
the ~15 people quietly stuck on the same thing.

Mechanics:
- Attendees work muted with video off. Nobody performs.
- **Questions go in a shared doc, not Zoom chat.** Chat scrolls away, dies with the call,
  and is useless on playback. A doc persists, everyone sees the whole list, and it becomes
  a written FAQ you can publish afterward. You read from it and answer aloud.
- Invite unmuting for anything worth hearing in a real voice — it records far better than
  you narrating someone else's typing.
- Screen share stays on your machine, parked on the recipe list, so anyone who drifts can
  re-find their place.
- Offer one breakout room as an opt-in "I want to talk it through" space only if a
  co-host materializes. Do not open five.

**Tradeoff to accept:** less peer-to-peer interaction than breakouts give. Worth it for
coverage when solo.

## The five recipes

Each is one page, assumes zero prior setup, and names the finish line so people know when
they're done rather than drifting.

### 1 · Agents — hand off one multi-step job
1. Open Gemini or ChatGPT and turn on the agent/deep-research mode.
2. Give it one real job from your own week. Template: *"Find three current, citable
   sources on [topic from your syllabus], published in the last two years. Summarize each
   in two sentences and tell me which is most appropriate for undergraduates."*
3. Let it run. Watch what it does rather than tabbing away — the visible steps are the
   point.
4. Check the sources actually exist. **This step is not optional.**

**Done when:** you have three sources you didn't have at 11:00, and you have personally
opened each one.

### 2 · Vibe Coding — one page into something interactive
1. Copy the text of one Canvas page you already have.
2. Paste it and say: *"Turn this into a self-check activity with 5 questions and immediate
   feedback for each answer. Give me a single HTML file."*
3. Preview it. Ask for one change in plain English. Watch it change.
4. Optional: paste into a Canvas page in HTML editor view.

**Done when:** you have a working interactive element you could put in front of students
this week.

### 3 · Your Course as a Folder — the flagship
1. Canvas → your course → **Settings → Export Course Content → Course → Create Export**.
2. Download the `.imscc`, rename the extension to `.zip`, unzip it. That folder is your
   course.
3. Open the OpenCode desktop app and point it at the folder.
4. Ask something no single page could answer: *"List every place my schedule and my
   syllabus disagree about a date."* Or: *"Which module pages don't have learning
   objectives?"*

**Done when:** you get one answer that would have taken you an hour of clicking.

> **Facilitator warnings.** The export takes a few minutes to generate — tell people to
> start it *first*, then read the rest while it runs. Work on a **copy** of the unzipped
> folder. And exported Canvas HTML is genuinely messy; set the expectation that this track
> is about *asking questions across the material*, not about producing clean edits to
> re-import.

### 4 · Gemini Notebook / Gem — grounded in your sources
**Notebook path:** add your syllabus and two readings as sources → ask three questions a
student would actually ask → click a citation and confirm it points somewhere real.

**Gem path:** new Gem → upload your unit materials → instruct it *"Only answer from the
files provided. If it isn't in them, say so."* → turn on Guided Learning → ask it
something the files don't cover and confirm it declines.

**Done when:** (Notebook) three answers with citations you traced, or (Gem) it correctly
refused a question outside your sources.

### 5 · Local AI — nothing leaves the laptop
1. Speech-to-text: install Handy, dictate a paragraph of feedback or an announcement.
2. Or text-to-speech: paste a reading into the browser TTS demo, generate audio.
3. Note the thing that matters: no account, no upload, works on a plane.

**Done when:** you have an audio or text file you produced with nothing leaving your
machine.

## Known risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Canvas export is slow or fails | Have your pre-exported folder ready to hand over |
| Nobody brought material | Starter pack — never let anyone sit out |
| Everyone picks the same track | Fine. Narrate that recipe and work it together |
| The doc goes quiet | Ask a direct question: "who has it open right now?" Seed it with 2 questions of your own before the session starts so it is never empty |
| Someone's agent hallucinates sources | Best teachable moment of the day — use it, don't hide it |
